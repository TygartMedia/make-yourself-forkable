# Make Yourself Forkable

I run an AI content factory. Twenty-plus WordPress sites, a cloud pipeline, packets engineered to be cited by machines. That is vibe production at industrial scale.

I also believe the machine cannot have been on the call — that ten thousand tiny judgments can't be copied, even by you from morning to afternoon — and that two contractors can rent the same model and cannot rent the harness built from their mistakes.

Both are true. They are only true if the line stays sharp. Generated syntax is exhaust. A well-structured packet is exhaust too, unless someone who has been on the call decided what belongs in it. The factory doesn't refute the argument. The factory *is* the argument, once you say the quiet part out loud: the output was never the asset. The judgment about what goes into it was.

This repo is the cluster of essays where I worked that out. Read them in order. They form one argument.

## The thesis, in five pieces

**1. The developer is a repo.**
[*The Price of a Life*](https://tygartmedia.com/the-price-of-a-life/) — You are not a code generator. You are a repo. Every outage, every impossible client, every 2am save: that's the commit history. Code is exhaust. The scars are the asset. The unit of value is wrong across the whole industry — stop pricing people per hour and start pricing them per merge. Citations, resolved jobs, leverage events: same unit.

**2. The senior operator is the source code. The model is the runtime.**
[*Restoration AI: The Senior Operator Is the Source Code*](https://tygartmedia.com/restoration-ai-source-code/) — The phrase is not a metaphor. The substance of what an AI system produces is determined by the captured judgment of the senior people whose decisions the system is trying to scale. Owners who treat AI as a technology purchase that reduces dependence on senior people get the predictable failure: the technology can't perform without the human substrate it required all along. Document the operator to amplify them, not to replace them — the snapshot ages the day they leave.

**3. Tacit knowledge is the last moat.**
[*Tacit Knowledge Is the Last Moat*](https://tygartmedia.com/tacit-knowledge-last-moat-only-defensible-asset-ai-era/) — Everything a model can learn from the internet is table stakes by definition. What's left is what was never written down: the judgment that lives in people's heads because no one ever asked them to say it out loud. That's the only defensible asset in the AI era, and it walks out the door every time a veteran retires.

**4. The floor rose. The ceiling did not.**
[*AI Raises the Floor, Not the Ceiling*](https://tygartmedia.com/ai-raises-the-floor-not-the-ceiling-restoration-industry-commentary/) — AI made the mediocre competent. It did not make the excellent obsolete — it made the excellent *legible* as the only thing left worth paying for. The gap between floor and ceiling is now the entire market.

**5. The test: drop something cold into the system and see if it can work without pinging the owner.**
[*The Cold-Start Test*](https://tygartmedia.com/cold-start-test-ai-model-readiness/) — Give a fresh model zero context and one job: find the work, route it, execute. If the score is high because the SOPs are good, say that. If the score is high because you're still the hidden runtime, say that too. A system that scores 8 or above doesn't need a smarter model — it needs live-state hygiene. Below that, you don't have an AI problem. You have a systems problem.
